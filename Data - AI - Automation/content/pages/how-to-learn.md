## The learning loop

Each lesson follows the same five steps. Do all five. Reading alone won't make you job-ready.

1. **Read** the lesson once from top to bottom. Don't take notes yet.
2. **Type** every example yourself in your own tool. Don't copy and paste.
3. **Try it yourself:** solve the exercise *before* opening the solution.
4. **Quiz:** click an answer to check it. You get a score out of 3, and your best score shows next to the lesson on its module page. If you miss one, re-read that section and press **Try again**.
5. **Job-ready check:** answer the interview question out loud, then do the mini project.

Then click **Mark lesson as complete**. Your progress is saved in this browser only; nothing is sent anywhere.

## Finding your way

- The **menu button ☰** (top left, or press `M`) shows or hides the full curriculum: phases, modules and lessons. It stays open while you move between lessons until you press the button again.
- **Search** (press `/`) finds lessons, glossary terms, tools and planned topics.
- **Glossary:** every lesson ends with its **Key terms**. Click one for a plain-English definition, or browse all terms on the [Glossary](glossary.html) page.
- Each lesson has **Previous / Next** buttons at the bottom and an **On this page** list on wide screens.

## Which path should I follow?

- **New to data?** Start at Phase 0, then Phase 1. Don't skip ahead.
- **Already use Excel or SQL at work?** Skim the lesson summaries and quizzes. If you pass a quiz easily, move on.
- **Targeting a specific job?** Pick a role track on the [home page](index.html) and follow its phases first.

!!! tip "The skip rule"
    Skip what you already know well. Review what you know weakly. Practise deeply what matters for your target job.

## Build evidence as you go

Employers hire on proof, not on certificates alone. Every phase ends with a **milestone project**. Keep each one in a public GitHub repository with a short README covering:

- the business question,
- what you built,
- the result (numbers, screenshots),
- what you would improve next.

## Use AI tools the right way

AI assistants are allowed, and you should use them. Follow two rules:

- **Ask the AI to explain, not just to answer.** "Why does this query need HAVING?" teaches you more than "fix my query".
- **Verify everything.** Run the code, check the numbers, and read the official docs. AI tools can be confidently wrong.

## Free resources

Every lesson ends with hand-picked free resources: official documentation, interactive exercises and free courses. Use them to go deeper after the lesson, not instead of it.

## Working offline

You can use this whole site without internet: download the repository and open `docs/index.html` in your browser. Search, the glossary, quiz scores, light/dark mode and progress tracking all work offline (scores and progress are saved in your browser only). Only the external resource links need internet.
